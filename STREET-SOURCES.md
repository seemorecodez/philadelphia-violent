# Mapped street objects

The street-object layer uses public Philadelphia/Pennsylvania data at its published coordinates. It does not procedurally scatter objects. Source queries omit credentials. Data availability and measured dimensions limit what can be displayed; a loaded data record is not proof that its model is visible.

Latest correction: Frank rejected the plain-ground replacement recorded in GROUND-CLEANUP.md because it lost photographic detail. The game again uses the original detailed imagery; selective cleanup is unfinished. RECONSTRUCTION-TARGET.md governs future reconstruction. Removing a slab does not excavate terrain or remove the image beneath it.

## Sources and dimensions

| Object | Public feature layer | Source fields used | Display limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trees | [PPR tree inventory 2025](https://services.arcgis.com/fLeGjb7u4uXqeF9q/arcgis/rest/services/ppr_tree_inventory_2025/FeatureServer/0) | `objectid`, point geometry, `tree_name`, `tree_dbh`, `year` | Snapshot of trees inventoried by PPR. No measured height or crown dimensions. `tree_dbh` is retained raw because its unit was not verified. |
| Poles | [Philadelphia Street Poles](https://services.arcgis.com/fLeGjb7u4uXqeF9q/ArcGIS/rest/services/Street_Poles/FeatureServer/0) | `objectid`, point geometry, `pole_num`, `type`, `height`, `nlumin`, `bulb_type` | Actual pole locations and classes. Arm bearing, diameter and luminaire dimensions are not supplied; the displayed form is a class-based approximation. |
| Sidewalks | [DVRPC Pedestrian Network](https://arcgis.dvrpc.org/portal/rest/services/transportation/pedestriannetwork_lines/FeatureServer/0) | `objectid`, polyline geometry, `line_type=1`, `width`, `material`, `captured` | Paths, not surveyed slab polygons. The width alias explicitly says **Width (Feet)**. Paths with no positive width are absent by default. |

[Philadelphia tree inventory catalog](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/philadelphia-tree-inventory/) and [Street Poles catalog](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/street-poles/) identify the City of Philadelphia license. Source attribution: **City of Philadelphia, Philadelphia Parks & Recreation; Philadelphia Streets Department**. These are snapshots/operational records, not a guarantee that every present-day street object is mapped.

The [DVRPC catalog](https://catalog.dvrpc.org/dataset/dvrpc-pedestrian-network) states that the data may be shared internally and externally without a data-sharing agreement; it separately lists “No License Provided.” Attribute **Delaware Valley Regional Planning Commission (DVRPC), Pedestrian Network**. Its [methodology](https://walk.dvrpc.org/about/) describes aerial mapping and subsequent maintenance, not a city-wide measured sidewalk mesh.

The [PASDA pole field documentation](https://pasda.psu.edu/uci/FullMetadataDisplay.aspx?file=PhillyStreets_Street_Poles2025.xml) explicitly defines `C13` and `C20` as 13-foot and 20-foot traffic posts. The `HEIGHT` description does not specify its unit. Other populated pole heights are interpreted as feet and tagged `source-height-unit-assumed`; this remains a documented uncertainty. Missing heights are skipped. Pole diameters, arms and light housings are display estimates. The app does not claim individual fixture reconstruction.

Sidewalk thickness is a display/gameplay estimate of 0.08 m. The initial implementation anchors a whole path to loaded terrain at its first vertex; it does not reconstruct surveyed longitudinal slope. `sidewalkWidthFallback` exists for explicit experiments but defaults to null and is not enabled. Trees require a licensed mesh supplied through `treeModelUrl`/`treeModelHeight`; until one is configured, tree records create neither geometry nor invisible colliders. A representative tree model does not establish actual species, canopy shape or height at an inventoried location.

## Rendering and gameplay contract

`createStreetObjects(viewer, options)` supplies `update(longitude, latitude)`, `getCollision(from, to, shape)`, `tryDestroy(id)`, `damageNearest(state, range=4)`, `tick(dt)`, `setVisible(value)`, `clear()`, `destroy()`, and `getStats()`.

Each 100 m navigation cell fetches a 300 m surrounding window. There is one active window, three sequential source requests, at most 160 records per source, and at most 300 retained objects. Only objects within 200 m and with loaded terrain plus usable dimensions can display. Individual requests stop after 12 seconds; successful windows have a 2.5-second cooldown and failures a 60-second cooldown. Truncated source responses are reported as incomplete coverage. Creation is paced to eight starts per half second; permanent model failures use the same 60-second retry pause. This is intentionally not exhaustive downloading.

Detailed trees have an additional default cap of the nearest **12 within 80 m** (`maxVisibleTrees`, `treeDisplayRadius`). A falling tree occupies a slot until removed. Moving changes the selected population; dropped trees lose both rendering and collision. This preserves source-model detail at a bounded GPU cost; it is not complete visible tree coverage. The renderer accepts at most 32 trees even when configured otherwise.

Poles and trunks have swept CPU colliders, enabled only after their visible primitive is ready. Sidewalks are traversable and can only be removed explicitly; they do not create barriers across the car path. Damage to a pole/tree disables its collision immediately, tips its model, and removes it after four simulation seconds. This is a reversible session game effect, not structural physics. Destroyed IDs persist while travelling or indoors until `clear()` starts a new session. `setVisible(false)` hides geometry, suspends collision/loading, and retains session damage. Late async responses/models are rejected and disposed after clearing or disconnecting.

These source layers contain neither building interiors nor structural damage models. The current textured downtown I3S layer remains the existing Philadelphia city layer; the limited alternative-source review did not verify a richer, legally reusable textured replacement.

## Mapped polygon sidewalks

The separate `src/sidewalk-surfaces.js` layer uses [Philadelphia Impervious Surfaces 2015, PASDA layer 11](https://mapservices.pasda.psu.edu/server/rest/services/pasda/CityPhilly/MapServer/11), queried with `FCODE=2210`. The [City catalog](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/impervious-surfaces/) lists the City of Philadelphia License and Public Use; Free. Preserve credit to **City of Philadelphia; PASDA; Pictometry Intelligent Images; Intergraph Services Company; Sanborn Mapping Services**. Source information and the larger city-detail limits are in `CITY-DETAIL-NEXT.md`.

The source supplies actual 2D boundaries and holes, rather than widths derived from pedestrian paths. `OBJECTID_1` identifies source polygons. Rings are grouped by containment and triangulated with their holes. Clipping those triangles into a stable 6 m grid preserves their footprint while making each section individually removable. The grid is a gameplay division, not surveyed pavement joints. Thickness **0.08 m** and plain concrete colour are estimates; exact pavement materials, curb profiles and ramp geometry are not available in this layer. Every generated vertex samples loaded terrain; missing terrain keeps the section absent. Four retained sections are checked every half simulation second, with at most 16 recorded vertex/anchor probes each. A change over 4 cm triggers a full vertex resample and rebuild, preserving the updated slope. The old mesh remains until its hidden replacement is ready. Missing new terrain retains the last valid surface; destroyed sections are never rebuilt. At the 96-section cap, a round of checks takes roughly 12 simulation seconds; larger per-section sample sets require additional rounds. Loaded terrain is an approximate ground surface, not a curb survey, and may differ from the 2015 polygon collection and 2023 aerial imagery.

API: `createSidewalkSurfaces(viewer, { onStatus, onDestroyed })` returns `update(longitude,latitude)`, `tick(dt)`, `setVisible(value)`, `tryDestroy(id)`, `damageNearest(state,range=4)`, `getCollision()`, `getStats()`, `clear()`, and `destroy()`. Collision always returns false; only ready, visible sections can receive manual damage. Damage removes a single section and persists for the session until `clear()`. Hiding stops loading and disables damage while retaining loaded source data. Clearing/disconnecting aborts pending queries and disposes late geometry.

One 140 m source envelope is queried per 40 m navigation cell, at most once every 2.5 seconds, with at most 80 source features and 60,000 processed vertices. Failed requests retry after 60 seconds. Requests use `credentials: 'omit'` and a 12-second timeout. The nearest **96 sections** are retained; geometry displays only within **90 m** and starts at most four sections per quarter second. `limited` reports truncation or the population cap. This bounded display is incomplete coverage. An enabled legacy DVRPC corridor layer should be disabled when the polygon layer is used to avoid duplicate slabs.

The verified spawn query returned 33 features / 57 rings / 24 holes. A one-shot Node processing check produced 606 clipped sections, selected 96 with source-boundary distances 9.67–55.81 m, and built 6,811 triangles. Parsing/clipping took 68.7 ms and construction 29.4 ms on this run; the latter used an explicit flat test terrain solely to exercise mesh construction. These are not browser frame/GPU timings or actual terrain validation. Runtime parsing yields between source batches, and display construction is paced. Nine focused tests cover rings/holes/islands, stable small sections, area preservation, terrain validity, request bounds, visible-only damage, stale-work cleanup, and bounded terrain re-anchoring with slope changes and pending-mesh disposal. Live visual quality remains separate from these checks.

`createDebris(viewer).addSidewalk(section)` adds a brief effect for a removed polygon section. It cuts at most 12 displayed triangular fragments from the section's existing clipped pieces, preserving source holes; their longest edges are at most 1.35 m. The fragments use the same terrain-sampled 8 cm geometry with closed undersides and computed lighting normals. A separate global pool holds at most 24 sidewalk fragments. The authored motion lifts them by less than 0.37 m, moves them sideways by less than 0.14 m, settles them, and fades them out at 2.4 simulation seconds. Readiness gates their animation lifetime; an unready fragment is removed after five simulation seconds. Hiding freezes the effect, and clearing removes pending and active pieces.

This effect is limited animated debris, not a material-conserving fracture or structural-physics simulation. It neither excavates terrain nor edits the source aerial photograph: the photographed sidewalk remains underneath after the 3D overlay is removed. Five focused debris tests check mapped-boundary/hole preservation, fragment size, closed geometry and normals, motion bounds, readiness, population caps, hiding, expiry, and cleanup. Live appearance still requires visual verification.
